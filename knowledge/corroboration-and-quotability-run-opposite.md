# Corroboration and quotability run opposite

Internal method knowledge (a reference note). Measured 2026-09-01 by the
assembler, on the first two event articles built either side of the claim-review
work. It reverses the obvious reading of a page, so it is worth knowing before
anyone judges an article by how much it quotes.

    Socorro   39 reviewed claims, 5 records    557 words, 27 citations,  3 quotes
    AAV      127 unreviewed,      2 records    916 words, 38 citations, 43 quotes

The better-sourced page is the shorter one and quotes a fourteenth as much.

## Why, and why it is structural rather than a coincidence

An event that several independent authors have written about is an event
documented in **books**, and books in this corpus are overwhelmingly copyright
`restricted` - their verbatim text may not be redistributed, so the publish step
withholds the excerpt and the article can cite but not quote.

An event that can be quoted at length is usually one carried by a **single public
document** - an incident report, a released transcript, a government paper. That
is exactly the source profile that scores POORLY on corroboration and
independence, because one document is one provenance root however long it is.

So the two axes are not merely uncorrelated. They pull against each other:

- many independent sources -> books -> restricted -> few quotes
- one quotable public document -> one root -> weak corroboration -> many quotes

## What follows

- **Quote count is not a quality signal, and is weakly inverse.** A page with
  forty quotations looks better evidenced and is not. Nothing in the pipeline
  should rank, gate, or present pages by quotation volume.
- **A thin, heavily-attributed article is the good outcome.** "According to X",
  "corroborated in substance by Y", "a separate account in Z describes" is what
  five reviewed sources buys, and it is the thing the claims-fusion design exists
  to make possible. It reads sparser than a page built from block quotes.
- **Expect the shape to spread.** As more nodes get reviewed, reviewed
  multi-source events will tend to be book-sourced and quote-poor. That is the
  corpus telling the truth about its own evidence, not a regression.

  records is not corroboration, and [[claims-fusion-as-robust-slam]] for the
  weighting this feeds.
